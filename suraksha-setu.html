Constitutional Bastion

The constitutional foundations of dignity, equality and safety

Articles 14, 15, 19 and 21 are Fundamental Rights; Article 39(d) is a Directive Principle of State Policy. These provisions provide context but do not all operate in the same way.

Article 14 · 15 · 19 · 21

Equality, non-discrimination, freedoms and personal liberty

Understand the broad constitutional principles that inform fairness, dignity and protection. Application depends on the specific provision and circumstances.

Article 39(d)

Equal pay for equal work

A Directive Principle directing the State towards equal pay for men and women for equal work. Directive Principles are not enforceable in the same way as Fundamental Rights.

Criminal-law context

Understanding the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023

The BNS is India's principal substantive criminal code and replaced the Indian Penal Code (IPC) from 1 July 2024, subject to applicable transitional and savings provisions. It defines offences and punishments; it is not the procedural code or evidence law.

BNS, 2023

Substantive criminal law: offences, definitions and punishments. It replaced the IPC for new-law purposes from 1 July 2024.

BNSS, 2023

Criminal procedure: processes such as investigation, arrest, trial and related procedures. It replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure, subject to applicable rules.

BSA, 2023

Evidence law: rules concerning evidence in judicial proceedings. It replaced the Indian Evidence Act, subject to applicable transitional provisions.

How this relates to POSH and POCSO: The BNS does not replace the POSH Act or the POCSO Act. POSH provides a specific workplace prevention and complaint-redressal framework. POCSO is a special child-protection law with its own offences, reporting duties and child-friendly procedures. Where facts may engage more than one law, trained authorities and legal professionals assess how the laws interact.

A brief timeline

From IPC and Vishaka to POSH, POCSO and the new criminal laws

A high-level timeline to understand how criminal law, constitutional principles and workplace-specific safeguards developed. These milestones are related but do not form a simple one-law replacement chain.

1860

Indian Penal Code (IPC)

The IPC became the principal general criminal code for India for many decades.

13 August 1997

Vishaka v. State of Rajasthan

The Supreme Court laid down workplace sexual-harassment guidelines grounded in constitutional rights, to operate until legislation was enacted.

14 November 2012

POCSO Act in force

A special legal framework for offences against children, including reporting duties and child-friendly procedures, came into force.

9 December 2013

POSH Act in force

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 established a statutory prevention and complaint-redressal framework.

25 December 2023 / 1 July 2024

New criminal laws enacted and commenced

BNS, BNSS and BSA received assent on 25 December 2023 and came into force on 1 July 2024, subject to their provisions and applicable transitional rules.

Quick reference

POSH–POCSO Glossary

Search common terms used in the learning modules. Definitions are simplified for education and should be checked against the Act for legal use.

Aggrieved woman

Under the POSH Act, the woman who alleges sexual harassment at the workplace. Check Section 2(a) and the applicable circumstances.

BNS

Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023: the substantive criminal code in force from 1 July 2024, subject to applicable provisions.

BNSS

Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023: the criminal procedure law.

BSA

Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023: the evidence law.

Child (POCSO)

A person below eighteen years of age under Section 2(1)(d) of the POCSO Act.

Confidentiality

Restricting disclosure of sensitive complaint and identity information to what the law permits and the process requires. POSH and POCSO have distinct rules.

Conciliation

Under POSH Section 10, a pre-inquiry process that may be attempted only at the request of the aggrieved woman; monetary settlement cannot be its basis.

Disclosure

When a person shares information about a possible incident. Receive it calmly, avoid blame and leading questions, and follow the required reporting pathway.

Grooming

A pattern of trust-building, manipulation, gifts, secrecy or pressure used to exploit a child. An awareness term, not a standalone POCSO offence label.

IC / Internal Committee

The workplace committee constituted by the employer under POSH Section 4 where the Act requires it.

LC / Local Committee

A committee constituted by the District Officer under POSH Section 6 for complaints within its statutory remit.

Quid pro quo

A Latin phrase meaning “this for that”; in a harassment context it can describe linking a work benefit or detriment to unwanted sexual conduct.

Reasonable apprehension

A statutory phrase relevant to POCSO Section 19 reporting; people should not wait for personal investigation or proof before following the required reporting process.

SJPU

Special Juvenile Police Unit, named in POCSO reporting provisions.

Trauma-informed response

A response that prioritises safety, dignity, calm listening, non-blaming language and avoidance of unnecessary repeated retelling.

Vishaka guidelines

Supreme Court guidelines laid down in 1997 for workplace sexual-harassment prevention and redressal until legislation was enacted.

Workplace (POSH)

The statutory definition in Section 2(o) includes specified workplaces and employment-related locations; it is not limited to an employee's desk.
