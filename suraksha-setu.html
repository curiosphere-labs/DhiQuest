Constitutional Bastion

The constitutional foundations of dignity, equality and safety

These provisions provide important constitutional context for equality, liberty and protection. They do not all operate in the same way: Articles 14, 15, 19 and 21 are Fundamental Rights, while Article 39(d) is a Directive Principle of State Policy.

Article 14

Equality before law

Requires equality before the law and equal protection of the laws. In safety and grievance systems, rules should be applied fairly and without arbitrary treatment.

Article 15

Non-discrimination

Prohibits specified forms of discrimination by the State, including on grounds of sex, and permits certain special provisions. It forms part of the wider constitutional commitment to equality.

Article 19

Freedoms

Protects specified freedoms for citizens, subject to the restrictions written into the Constitution. It should not be described as an unrestricted right to say or do anything.

Article 21

Life and personal liberty

No person may be deprived of life or personal liberty except according to procedure established by law. Courts have interpreted this guarantee in ways that include dignity and privacy.

Article 39(d)

Equal pay for equal work

A Directive Principle directing the State towards equal pay for men and women for equal work. Article 37 says Directive Principles are not enforceable by courts in the same way as Fundamental Rights, although they are fundamental to governance.

Criminal-law context

Understanding the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS), 2023

The BNS is India's principal substantive criminal code that replaced the Indian Penal Code (IPC) from 1 July 2024, subject to applicable transitional and savings provisions. It defines offences and punishments; it is not the procedural code or evidence law.

BNS, 2023

Substantive criminal law: offences, definitions and punishments. It replaced the IPC for new-law purposes from 1 July 2024.

BNSS, 2023

Criminal procedure: processes such as investigation, arrest, trial and related procedural matters. It replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure, subject to applicable rules.

BSA, 2023

Evidence law: rules concerning evidence in judicial proceedings. It replaced the Indian Evidence Act, subject to applicable transitional provisions.

How this relates to POSH and POCSO

The BNS does not replace the POSH Act or the POCSO Act. POSH provides a specific workplace prevention and complaint-redressal framework. POCSO is a special child-protection law with its own offences, reporting duties and child-friendly procedures. Where facts may engage more than one law, trained authorities and legal professionals assess how the laws interact.

Frequently Asked Questions: POSH & POCSO

These answers provide general awareness, not a substitute for legal advice or current official guidance.

1. What does POCSO cover, and who is a child?

The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act, 2012 addresses specified sexual offences against children and provides child-sensitive procedures. For the Act, a child is a person below 18 years of age. Consult the current Act for definitions and offence-specific provisions.

2. Is POCSO gender-neutral?

Yes. The Act's definition of child is not restricted by gender, and its protections apply to children of any gender.

3. What should a school employee do after a child discloses possible sexual abuse?

Stay calm, listen without blame or leading questions, avoid promising secrecy, preserve the child's dignity and safety, and promptly follow the mandatory reporting process under Section 19 by reporting to the Special Juvenile Police Unit or local police. Do not conduct an informal investigation or attempt mediation instead of reporting.

4. Can a school resolve a suspected POCSO offence internally?

An internal meeting or disciplinary process must not be used as a substitute for statutory reporting. Section 19 reporting obligations should be followed where applicable; seek guidance from the designated authorities and qualified counsel.

5. Must every adult report a suspected POCSO offence?

Section 19 imposes a reporting duty on a person who has apprehension that an offence under the Act is likely to be committed or knowledge that it has been committed. Section 21 addresses punishment for failure to report or record, subject to the provision's terms and exceptions.

6. What is the POSH Act?

The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 creates a framework to prevent and prohibit workplace sexual harassment and provide a redressal mechanism for complaints covered by the Act.

7. What is the difference between an Internal Committee and a Local Committee?

The employer constitutes an Internal Committee in workplaces covered by Section 4. The District Officer constitutes a Local Committee under Section 6 to receive complaints from specified workplaces or situations, including where an establishment has fewer than ten workers or where the complaint is against the employer. Check the exact statutory conditions before advising a person.

8. When is an Internal Committee required?

Section 4 requires an employer to constitute an Internal Committee by written order at each office or administrative unit of a workplace with ten or more workers. The full statutory composition and nomination requirements should be checked in the Act.

9. What is the usual POSH complaint filing period?

Section 9 generally provides a period of three months from the incident, or in a series of incidents from the last incident; the Committee may extend the period by up to a further three months for reasons recorded in writing where the circumstances prevented filing within the initial period. Seek advice promptly rather than assuming a deadline has passed.

10. What are the key POSH inquiry timelines?

Under the Act, the inquiry is to be completed within 90 days; the Committee provides its report within 10 days of completion, and the employer or District Officer acts on the recommendations within 60 days. Specific steps and applicability should be verified against the Act and Rules.

11. Is conciliation compulsory under POSH?

No. Under Section 10, conciliation may be initiated before inquiry only at the request of the aggrieved woman. A monetary settlement cannot be the basis of conciliation. The Committee should not pressure a complainant to choose it.

12. Can details of a POSH inquiry be shared widely?

Section 16 restricts publication or communication of complaint contents, identities and inquiry proceedings, subject to the Act. Information should be shared only as permitted and necessary for the process; avoid circulating details through informal chats or public channels.

13. Does POSH cover work-related travel, events or digital communication?

The statutory definition of workplace in Section 2(o) is not limited to a person's desk. It includes specified places visited arising out of or during employment and employer-provided transportation. Assess the facts and statutory definition rather than assuming an off-site or online incident is automatically outside the framework.

14. Does completing this course make someone a statutory expert or guarantee compliance?

No. This is an awareness and learning programme. It does not confer a government licence, replace specialist legal advice, or guarantee that an organisation complies with all legal obligations.

15. How does certificate issuance work?

Complete the learning modules, submit the relevant assessment, and wait for DhiQuest to review the result. If you meet the pass criteria, you receive payment instructions for the course's certificate fee. The certificate is prepared and shared manually after payment is confirmed.
